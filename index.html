About

The GLOG Workshop is a day-long workshop aiming to share some of the core works serving as the backbone of the Global Log (GLOG) project. The workshop includes the presentation of GLOG's recently accepted papers, invited top researchers across Portuguese universities, and also has the honor of hosting the distinguished lecture for one of our key partners and consultants, Robbert van Renesse.

Distinguished Lecture

Distinguished Lecture

Chain Replication Revisited

Robbert van Renesse

Cornell University · 12:00 – 13:00, Anfiteatro Abreu Faro

Abstract

Chain Replication tolerates f crash failures with only f+1 replicas, half of what Paxos needs, and is used in production storage systems such as EBS, Delta and RADOS. This talk revisits Chain Replication twenty years after its introduction. Quorum protocols such as Paxos never need to tell a slow process from a crashed one. Our new protocol, Chain Replication Revisited (CR2), takes the opposite approach: it makes slowness indistinguishable from a crash. Replicas hold leases from a small set of lessors and stop themselves when they can no longer renew them. Lessors keep no persistent state and never communicate, and the only assumption is a bound on clock drift. With failure detection that is guaranteed accurate, the chain can manage its own membership without a consensus service. CR2 also persists updates in parallel rather than one replica at a time and can recover even when every replica crashes (assuming stable storage).

Biography

Robbert van Renesse is a Professor of Computer Science at Cornell University. His research focuses on fault-tolerant and scalable distributed systems. He co-designed chain replication and has worked on systems ranging from Amoeba to Ziplog. He has also co-founded several companies, including Reliable Network Solutions and Exostellar, bringing systems research into practice. He received his Ph.D. from the Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam under Andrew S. Tanenbaum. He is an ACM Fellow and co-editor-in-chief of ACM Transactions on Computer Systems.
